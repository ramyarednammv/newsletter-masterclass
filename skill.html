Thirteen files, about 20,000 words: the positioning method, your four pillars, the seven story beats, every format spec'd to the word, the subject-line data, the growth mechanics, the metrics that matter, and the deliverability rules. Everything from both sessions.

Add it in three steps

  1. Download the file using the button above. It'll land in your Downloads folder.
  2. In Claude, go to Settings → Capabilities → Skills and choose Upload skill.
  3. Upload the file. Done. Claude now uses it automatically whenever you mention your newsletter — you don't have to ask it to.

Skills need a paid Claude plan. If you don't see the Skills option, use the three prompts — same system, you just paste them each time.

What to say to it once it's in

No prompts, no copying. Talk to it the way you'd talk to someone who already knows your business:

"Help me write this week's issue."

"I just got off a call with a client — here's what she said. Is there an issue in this?"

"Nobody's opening my emails. What am I doing wrong?"

"Turn this voice note into a newsletter." (then paste the transcript)

"Read this draft and tell me honestly whether I should send it."

"I've run out of things to write about."

"Give me four subject lines for this and tell me which one you'd send."

"Plan my next three months of issues."

It'll ask you questions one at a time, with lettered options if you're stuck, and it won't invent a single number or client detail you haven't given it.

The rules it will never break

  • It never invents a number, a client, a quote or a detail you didn't give it. If it needs something, it asks. One made-up detail would contaminate everything you ever publish
  • It never writes your humour. It leaves you a [HUMAN: …] marker instead — a joke written by a machine lands one time in twenty, and the other nineteen tell your reader who wrote it
  • It never uses a client's name. "A client asked me last week" is always enough
  • It never makes you the hero. The version where you got it wrong first is the version people trust
  • It never sends anything. It drafts; you send. Your name is on it
